# Custom software (https://neon1.tech/)

Web applications, portals, internal tools, APIs, and services built for a need off-the-shelf software doesn't meet: when it's worth building, what I deliver, and what you keep.

## When it's worth building

- No product does what the work needs, or the closest one needs so many workarounds that people avoid it.
- Customers or staff need a portal, form, or dashboard tied to your own data.
- A spreadsheet or script runs something important and has outgrown its owner.
- Several tools could be replaced by one small application that fits how you work.
- Another system needs an API or service that doesn't exist yet.

## What I do

- **Web applications and portals.** Customer, client, and staff-facing applications with sign-in, roles, and the screens people need, built to work on a phone as well as a desk.
- **Internal tools.** Admin screens, intake and approval flows, and dashboards that replace spreadsheets and email chains.
- **APIs and services.** Documented APIs, background jobs, and integrations other systems can rely on.
- **AI features.** Search, summaries, drafting, and extraction over your own documents, with access control, cited sources, and checks against real examples.
- **Delivery.** Tests, a release pipeline, monitoring, and a runbook, so the software keeps working after handover.

## What you get

- A short requirements and design record that says what the software does and what it doesn't.
- The working application in your accounts, with its source code in your repository.
- Tests against the agreed acceptance criteria, including failure cases.
- Release, monitoring, and recovery steps written down for whoever runs it next.

## What it needs from you

- A person who knows the work the software supports and can answer questions quickly.
- Examples of the real inputs, outputs, and edge cases.
- Access to the systems and accounts it connects to.
- Time to try it at each milestone.

## Build only what's needed

I check first whether a product, a configuration change, or an [automation](use-case-workflows.md) covers the need, because that usually costs less to own. When building is right, I choose common, well-supported tools, so the code stays maintainable by you or another developer. You own the code, the accounts, and the data.
