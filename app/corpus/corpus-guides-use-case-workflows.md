# Workflow automation (https://neon1.tech/)

Integrating business systems, automating processes, and building the software in between: when it's worth doing, what I deliver, and how I choose the right tool.

## When it's worth doing

- Two or more systems of record disagree, such as inventory, orders, customers, or invoices, and exceptions are reconciled by hand.
- A vendor sync or script breaks quietly, and nobody owns it.
- Reporting needs data from several systems that don't export cleanly.
- Approvals, reconciliation, or handoffs span teams or tools.
- Documents arrive in volume and need structured extraction with human review.
- A customer-facing or internal application is needed beside the existing stack.

## What I do

- **Integration.** APIs, webhooks, and event-driven sync between CRM, ERP, point-of-sale, accounting, scheduling, and document systems, with identifiers mapped and a clear source of truth for each record.
- **Exception handling.** Retries, idempotency, reconciliation reports, and a queue for anything that needs a person, so failures are visible rather than silent.
- **Custom applications.** Portals, internal tools, and services where configuration and integration can't meet the requirement.
- **Reporting and visibility.** Data consolidated into dashboards and reports that people use.
- **AI where it earns its place.** Document classification and extraction with review, measured against real samples.

## What you get

An integration map and data ownership rules, a working implementation in your accounts, tests against agreed acceptance criteria including failure cases, monitoring, and a runbook for each integration.

## What it needs from you

Admin access to the systems involved, vendor API terms and limits, a person who knows the process, and sample data that shows the exceptions as well as the normal case.

## The lightest tool that works

I start with what you already own: a native integration or marketplace app, then an integration platform such as n8n, Zapier, or Make, which I recommend and can set up. Where volume is low, a documented manual step costs less than software. Custom code covers the rest. Your working systems of record stay in place, and your accounts and data stay yours. Vendor API limits and data quality shape the design, so discovery checks both before the estimate is set.
