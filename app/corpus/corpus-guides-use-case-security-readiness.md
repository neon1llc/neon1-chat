# Security readiness (https://neon1.tech/)

Assessing technical risk, strengthening controls, and preparing systems for a security review or audit: what I deliver, and where an auditor or specialist takes over.

## When it's worth doing

- A customer or insurer sent a security questionnaire, and the answers need to be true.
- A SOC 2, HIPAA, or PCI DSS review is coming, and controls or evidence aren't ready.
- Access has grown loose: shared accounts, missing MFA, admin rights nobody reviews, or slow offboarding.
- Backups exist but a restore has never been tested.
- A cloud account or network has never had an independent configuration review.
- A new system or vendor is about to handle sensitive data.

## What I do

- **Technical risk assessment.** What's exposed, what matters most, and what to fix first, using the [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) functions as a shared vocabulary.
- **Identity and access.** MFA, least privilege, named accounts, access reviews, and offboarding.
- **Hardening.** Cloud, network, and endpoint configuration checked against vendor baselines and fixed.
- **Data protection and recovery.** Encryption that fits the data, independent backups, and timed restore tests.
- **Control and evidence work.** Controls implemented and documented, and evidence gathered in the form the auditor or customer expects.
- **Incident preparedness.** Contacts, decisions, and first steps written down and rehearsed.

## What you get

Prioritized findings, a remediation plan, the changes made and verified, and evidence of the checks performed.

## When something else fits better

- You need an audit opinion. That comes from an independent auditor, such as a CPA firm for SOC 2.
- You need ongoing evidence collection across many controls. A compliance platform such as Vanta, Drata, or Secureframe may fit, and I can help set it up.
- You need a penetration test, 24/7 monitoring, or incident response in progress. A specialist or managed security provider fits better.
- You need legal advice on an obligation. A lawyer does that, and I'll work with them on the technical side.
